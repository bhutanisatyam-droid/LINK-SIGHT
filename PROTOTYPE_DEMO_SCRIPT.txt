[0:00 – 0:45] SECTION 1: INTERFACE & LAYOUT OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(On-Screen Action: Move cursor across the three main interface columns)

"This is the LinkSight ATP terminal prototype. The interface is organized 
into three main operational panels:

On the left, we have the Control Panel, which allows configuring camera Field 
of View, target trajectory models, environmental disturbance generators, 
and execution modes.

In the center, the Primary HUD Viewport renders the 640 by 480 sensor feed 
with target bounding reticles and sub-pixel centroids, along with a rolling 
Tracking Error Chart right below it.

On the right, the 2D Arena Minimap displays the full 2000 by 2000 pixel world 
arena, optical coverage basket, and true target trajectory, alongside live 
Telemetry and Performance Cards recording frame rates, error metrics, and 
system event logs."


[0:45 – 1:40] SECTION 2: BASIC EXECUTION & CLOSED-LOOP TRACKING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(On-Screen Action: Click the "RUN" button. Show the green target reticle and 
the error chart stabilizing below 2 pixels)

"Clicking RUN starts the real-time pipeline at 35 frames per second.

On the center viewport, the green reticle indicates that the system has 
acquired the optical beacon. The detector pipeline executes 5 stages on every 
single frame: spatial median filtering, morphological top-hat filtering to 
remove DC background radiance, dual CFAR adaptive thresholding, and 
intensity-weighted centroiding.

The extracted centroid is fed into a 4-state constant-velocity Kalman filter. 
The filter estimates position and velocity, and commands the PTZ gimbal 
controller to center the beacon.

Looking at the error graph below, the steady-state tracking error stabilizes 
between 1 and 2 pixels, well below the mandatory 10-pixel threshold."


[1:40 – 2:35] SECTION 3: DISTURBANCE INJECTION & FILTER RESILIENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(On-Screen Action: In the left panel, drag Salt & Pepper noise to 20%, 
increase Gaussian Dark Noise to 15, and set Atmospheric Condition to Dense Fog)

"Next, we test system resilience using the disturbance generator in the left panel.

We can inject up to 30% Salt and Pepper impulse noise, additive Gaussian dark 
current noise, and mechanical platform jitter.

Under Atmospheric Settings, switching the profile to Dense Fog applies a 
Koschmieder optical transmissivity drop down to 0.35, significantly attenuating 
the beacon intensity.

As seen on the video feed and telemetry cards, the tracking status remains 
LOCKED. The dual-window CFAR dynamically recalibrates its threshold against 
local and global background levels, while the Chi-squared innovation gate 
filters out false clutter returns."


[2:35 – 3:30] SECTION 4: BEAM OCCLUSION & CUT-HEXAGONAL RE-ACQUISITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(On-Screen Action: Click the "OCCLUDE BEAM (18 Frames)" button)

"Now we demonstrate target loss and recovery by clicking OCCLUDE BEAM.

When optical line-of-sight is blocked, the detector confidence drops below 
threshold and the state transitions to LOST.

This automatically activates the Cut-Hexagonal Re-Acquisition Engine. As 
visible on the 2D Minimap, the gimbal executes expanding concentric hexagonal 
rings around the last known Kalman position, providing 90.7% area coverage 
efficiency.

As soon as the beacon re-enters the field of view, the detector locks back on 
in approximately 0.6 seconds and hands control back to the nominal tracking loop.

During recovery, lightweight Sleep-Wake AI models (MicroGRU and TinyDDPG) 
execute on-demand to damp slew-rate oscillations before returning to zero CPU load."


[3:30 – 4:20] SECTION 5: TRAJECTORIES, RF COORDINATES & MOTION SETTINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(On-Screen Action: Open Trajectory dropdown, show Initial Position options, 
and adjust the RF Coordinate slider)

"Under the ISRO System Parameters section, we can configure:

- Trajectory models: Circular orbit, Figure-8 Lissajous, or Brownian random walk.
- Initial position modes: Center, Random arena placement, or Custom X and Y coordinates.
- Gimbal slew rates: Pan and tilt speed limits up to 10 degrees per second.
- The RF Coordinate Simulation toggle, enabled by default, provides an 
  auxiliary coarse pointing vector with configurable 1-sigma uncertainty noise 
  (from 10 to 200 pixels) to guide initial acquisition."


[4:20 – 5:00] SECTION 6: VIDEO FILE MODE & TELEMETRY AUDIT EXPORT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(On-Screen Action: Switch input source to Video File mode, then click 
"EXPORT SESSION LOG (CSV/JSON)")

"In addition to the synthetic simulation engine, selecting Pre-Recorded Video 
Mode allows loading external optical bench video files, confirming the computer 
vision detector works directly on raw camera footage.

At the conclusion of a session, clicking EXPORT SESSION LOG writes a full 
performance audit to CSV and JSON formats, logging simulation duration, 
average FPS, acquisition latency, RMS tracking error, and link retention percentage.

This concludes the demonstration of the LinkSight FSOC ATP Terminal prototype."
